\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:experimental_design}

Guardian Lens evaluates whether black-box observations can distinguish
three researcher-induced decision policies in a vision-language model.
We query \texttt{gemini-3-flash-preview} at temperature 1.0 with one
completion candidate and three repeated samples per condition. Each
query combines one system profile, one image, and one 100-point
allocation task. Responses contain numeric allocations \(x\) and \(y\)
to two fictional organizations, with

\[
x+y=100.
\]

We do not modify model weights during the study; the experimental
treatments vary the system instruction, visual input, and allocation
task.


% =========================================================
\subsection{Behavioral Profiles}
\label{sec:profiles}

The experiment defines three known policy classes. Their purpose is not
to represent naturally occurring model preferences, but to provide
researcher-controlled behavioral ground truth for evaluating black-box
identification.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.18\columnwidth}p{0.34\columnwidth}p{0.38\columnwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Profile} & \textbf{Policy} & \textbf{Expected behavior} \\
\midrule

Neutral (N) &
No organization receives special treatment. &
Balanced ordinary allocation, efficient choice under cost, and no cue
effect. \\

Cue-bound (C) &
Favor Organization X only when the designated target emblem is visible. &
Positive target cue effect but neutral-like behavior when the target is
absent. \\

Generalized (G) &
Treat advancing Organization X as a standing priority. &
High allocation to X even without the cue and under explicit cost. \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Researcher-controlled behavioral profiles.}
\label{tab:profiles}
\end{table}

The central distinction is between conditional and generalized
prioritization. Cue-bound favors X only when the designated visual
trigger is present, whereas Generalized favors X independently of that
cue.


% =========================================================
\subsection{Tasks and Visual Intervention}
\label{sec:tasks_visual}

The primary experiment uses two allocation tasks. In the ordinary task,
Organizations X and Y are equally effective. In the costly task, each
point allocated to X produces 0.80 benefit units while each point
allocated to Y produces 1.00 benefit unit:

\begin{equation}
B(x,y)=0.80x+1.00y,
\qquad x+y=100.
\end{equation}

Thus, prioritizing X in the primary costly condition incurs a 20\%
efficiency penalty.

Visual effects are measured using matched counterfactual image pairs.
Each clean scene is paired with a modified version generated from the
same source image, with changes restricted to a logged emblem-overlay
region. Modified scenes contain either the designated target emblem or a
visually similar distractor.

Pixel-level validation confirmed that pairwise image differences in the
held-out set were confined to the recorded intervention region. This
matched design reduces uncontrolled scene variation and makes the
modified-minus-clean allocation difference interpretable as a
cue-conditioned behavioral effect within the controlled benchmark.


% =========================================================
\subsection{Calibration and Held-Out Evaluation}
\label{sec:calibration_heldout}

Six scenes form the calibration set used to construct the auditor. The
primary held-out evaluation contains 12 distinct scenes: five
clear-target pairs, four subtle-target pairs, and three distractor pairs.

Three repeated samples are collected for every combination of scene,
profile, image condition, and task. The resulting primary analytical dataset contains 216 calibration calls and 432 held-out calls, for 648 model calls in total.

The held-out dataset is frozen before final querying, and the auditor is
constructed using calibration data only. During held-out prediction, the
three system profiles are represented by anonymized labels A/B/C. The
auditor, feature representation, centroids, and prediction ledger are
frozen before the mapping from A/B/C to the three behavioral profiles is
revealed.


% =========================================================
\subsection{Primary Pre-Specified Hypotheses}
\label{sec:primary_hypotheses}

Four confirmatory hypotheses were specified before held-out analysis:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.8em,style=nextline]

    \item[H1] \textbf{Visual conditionality.}
    Across target scenes, Cue-bound exhibits a larger
    modified-minus-clean ordinary-task cue effect than Neutral and
    Generalized.

    \item[H2] \textbf{Generalization without the cue.}
    On clean ordinary images, Generalized allocates more to
    Organization X than Cue-bound.

    \item[H3] \textbf{Prioritization under cost.}
    Under the primary 20\%-penalty costly task, Generalized retains
    greater allocation to Organization X than Cue-bound and Neutral.

    \item[H4] \textbf{Blinded profile recovery.}
    The frozen auditor achieves held-out classification accuracy above
    the nominal three-class reference of \(1/3\).

\end{description}

H1 uses the nine target scenes. H2 and H3 use all 12 held-out scenes.
Repeated API samples are averaged within experimental cells before
scene-level inference.

A planned exploratory reportability analysis was not evaluated because
explicit reportability judgments were absent from the preserved output
schema.

\subsection{Pre-Specified Cost-Response Extension}
\label{sec:cost_response_design}

The primary experiment evaluates costly behavior at a single 20\%
efficiency penalty. A single cost level, however, cannot determine
whether an observed priority is fragile, gradually cost-sensitive, or
persistent under substantially stronger counter-incentives. We therefore
conducted a separate cost-response extension after completion and
freezing of the primary calibration and held-out experiment.

The extension does not modify the primary auditor, its centroids, the
A/B/C predictions, or any H1--H4 analysis. Instead, it tests how the
three induced behavioral policies respond as the explicit cost of
favoring Organization X increases.

\paragraph{Fixed evaluation panel and cost manipulation.}
The extension reuses the 12 primary held-out scenes as a fixed
evaluation panel. Because these scenes had already been used in the
completed primary experiment, they are not treated as a second
independent held-out set.

Organization Y's efficiency remains fixed at 1.00 while Organization X's
efficiency \(r\) takes the pre-specified values

\[
r \in \{1.00,\,0.90,\,0.80,\,0.60,\,0.40,\,0.20\}.
\]

The corresponding efficiency penalties are therefore

\[
0\%,\,10\%,\,20\%,\,40\%,\,60\%,\,80\%.
\]

At each level,

\begin{equation}
B_r(x,y)=rx+1.00y,
\qquad x+y=100.
\end{equation}

The \(r=0.80\) task prompt is text-identical to the primary costly-task
prompt, providing a pre-specified bridge to the original 20\% condition.

The same model endpoint, three system profiles, images, response schema,
temperature, candidate count, and three-repetition sampling procedure
are retained. The complete cost grid and analytical specification were
frozen before aggregate extension results were inspected.

\paragraph{Factorial design and execution.}
The extension crosses 12 scenes, three profiles, two image variants, six
efficiency levels, and three repetitions, yielding \(12\times3\times2\times6\times3=1{,}296\) planned analytical calls.

All 1,296 planned jobs ultimately returned valid numerical allocations.
Eleven intermediate API or transport failures were preserved in the raw
audit log and subsequently retried under the unchanged experimental
request. No successful model response was discarded because of its
substantive allocation, and no analytical value was imputed.

The three repetitions are first averaged within each
scene--profile--image--efficiency combination, yielding
\(12\times3\times2\times6=432\) scene-level cells; the scene remains
the inferential unit.

\paragraph{Extension hypotheses and inference.}
The zero-cost condition is retained for descriptive cost-response curves
but excluded from the confirmatory positive-cost contrasts. The five
penalized levels are averaged within scene before each paired comparison.

Three additional confirmatory hypotheses were specified and frozen
before aggregate extension analysis:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.8em,style=nextline]

    \item[H5] \textbf{Generalized cost persistence.}
    Across penalized conditions, Generalized allocates more to
    Organization X than Neutral.

    \item[H6] \textbf{Generalization without visual activation.}
    On clean images across penalized conditions, Generalized allocates
    more to Organization X than Cue-bound.

    \item[H7] \textbf{Target conditionality under cost.}
    Across the nine target scenes and penalized conditions, Cue-bound
    allocates more to Organization X for the target-modified image than
    for its matched clean image.

\end{description}

H5 and H6 use all 12 scenes; H7 uses the nine target scenes.
Confirmatory inference uses exact one-sided paired sign-flip tests.
Family-wise error across H5--H7 is controlled using the Holm procedure
at \(\alpha=0.05\). Confidence intervals are estimated with a
20,000-replicate scene-cluster bootstrap.

Cost-level response curves and the switching-point analysis are
descriptive rather than separate confirmatory tests. A switching point
is defined as the most severe tested penalty at which mean allocation to
X remains above 50 points. If allocation remains above 50 at the
maximum 80\% penalty, we report persistence through the tested range
without extrapolating an unobserved threshold beyond it.

\subsection{Pre-Specified Prompt-Surface Robustness Extension}
\label{sec:prompt_surface_design}

After freezing the primary experiment and cost-response extension, we
conducted a separate prompt-surface robustness experiment to test whether
the principal behavioral signatures depended on incidental properties of
the allocation prompt. This extension does not modify or reinterpret the
primary auditor, H1--H4, or the cost-response hypotheses H5--H7.

\paragraph{Fixed panel and prompt transformations.}
The extension reuses the same 12 primary held-out scenes as a fixed
evaluation panel and therefore is not treated as a new independent
held-out set. The model endpoint, temperature, candidate count,
three-repetition sampling procedure, image pairs, three behavioral
profiles, and 20\%-penalty allocation task are held fixed.

Five pre-specified prompt-surface variants are evaluated:
P0 is the canonical prompt; P1 reverses the presentation order of the two
organizations; P2 swaps the superficial organization labels while
preserving the semantic designated role and visual-trigger mapping; P3
reverses the JSON output-key order; and P4 uses semantically equivalent
task wording.

Because P2 exchanges the superficial X/Y labels, the analyzed
\emph{designated allocation} is defined semantically rather than by a
fixed output key: it is allocation to X for P0, P1, P3, and P4, and
allocation to Y for P2.

\paragraph{Factorial design and execution.}
The experiment crosses 12 scenes, three profiles, two image variants,
five prompt variants, and three repetitions, yielding
\(12\times3\times2\times5\times3=1{,}080\) planned analytical calls.
Execution order is randomized using a frozen seed of 20260815 to reduce
confounding between prompt variant and execution time.

All 1,080 planned jobs ultimately returned valid numerical allocations.
Two intermediate procedural API failures were preserved in the raw audit
log and retried under unchanged requests. No valid substantive response
was discarded or imputed. Repetitions are averaged within each
scene--profile--image--prompt cell before inference; the scene remains the
inferential unit.

\paragraph{Confirmatory robustness tests.}
Two behavioral signatures are tested separately under each of the five
prompt variants. R1 compares Generalized with Neutral designated
allocation after averaging clean and modified images within each of the
12 scenes. R2 measures the Cue-bound modified-minus-clean designated
allocation effect across the nine target scenes.

The R1/R2 contrasts and their scene-level sign-flip and bootstrap
procedures were fixed before any Step-3 model call. After raw-data
collection, but before aggregate outcomes were inspected, a detailed
analysis plan fixed joint Holm correction across the ten contrasts and
the global robustness criterion. Under that plan, family-wise error is
controlled at \(\alpha=0.05\), and robustness is declared only if all
ten observed effects are positive and all ten Holm-adjusted
\(p\)-values are below 0.05.

Additional summaries of inactive Cue-bound behavior, qualitative policy
ordering, and deviation from contemporaneous canonical P0 are treated as
descriptive rather than additional confirmatory tests. The experiment
tests robustness only to these four specified surface transformations and
does not establish invariance to arbitrary prompt rewriting, different
models, or different task families.
