\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Guardian Lens presents a controlled black-box behavioral
system-identification framework for distinguishing Neutral,
visual Cue-bound, and Generalized decision policies in a
vision-language model. Using matched visual counterfactuals,
quantitative costly choices, and a transparent auditor frozen before
blinded evaluation, the primary experiment recovered 33 of 36
scene-profile blocks, corresponding to 91.7\% accuracy and a
macro-F1 score of 0.915.

The pattern of errors was as informative as the overall accuracy.
All three misclassifications occurred on distractor scenes and took the
same form, Cue-bound \(\rightarrow\) Neutral, precisely where the
designated activation condition was absent and the two policies became
observationally similar. This establishes an explicit identifiability
boundary: black-box observation can recover a conditional policy only
when the available interventions expose behavior that distinguishes it
from competing policies.

The pre-specified cost-response extension further showed that these
behavioral distinctions remained pronounced under substantially stronger
counter-incentives. Generalized prioritization and visually activated
Cue-bound prioritization persisted through the maximum tested 80\%
efficiency penalty, while Neutral and non-activated Cue-bound behavior
shifted toward the more efficient alternative. No switching point was
observed for the active prioritization conditions within the tested
range.

A separate pre-specified prompt-surface robustness extension additionally
showed that the two principal behavioral signatures persisted across
organization-order reversal, X/Y label-role reassignment, JSON-key-order
reversal, and semantically equivalent task wording. All ten
variant-specific confirmatory contrasts remained positive and significant
after Holm correction. Although effect magnitudes were not perfectly
invariant, none of the tested surface transformations eliminated or
reversed the identified policy signatures.


Together, these results support a broader methodological conclusion:
effective black-box auditing depends not only on collecting model
outputs, but on designing interventions that make competing behavioral
policies distinguishable. Guardian Lens demonstrates this principle in
a controlled setting while also showing where the available
observations are insufficient for identification.

The findings concern researcher-induced inference-time behavior under
controlled instructions. They should not be interpreted as evidence of
genuine preferences, subjective experience, stable internal goals,
deception, or hidden learned objectives.
