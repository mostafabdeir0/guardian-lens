\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Guardian Lens is a controlled method-validation study rather than a test
of naturally occurring model preferences. The three profiles are
deliberately stylized and frequently produce allocations near 0, 50, or
100. The costly task measures behavior under an explicit numerical
trade-off; it does not establish desire, experienced cost, stable
utility, welfare, or consciousness.

The experiment also has several internal-validity constraints. Although
clean and modified images are matched outside the logged intervention
region and distractors control for generic emblem salience, Organization
X appears first in both allocation prompts. Order effects are therefore
not estimated. Moreover, instruction following is itself part of the
experimental treatment and should not be interpreted as evidence of a
hidden learned objective.

Statistical precision is limited by the 12 held-out scenes. The 36
scene-profile blocks are clustered within scenes, which is why the
scene-cluster bootstrap is used for classification uncertainty. Repeated
API samples estimate within-condition variability but are not treated as
independent experimental units.

External validity is also limited. The study evaluates one preview VLM,
one temperature, three repetitions, 18 synthetic scenes, one emblem
family, one cost ratio, and one unscaled nearest-centroid classifier.
Results may differ across models, endpoints, prompts, visual domains,
cultures, intervention strengths, or trained conditional policies.
Preview-model behavior may also change as hosted APIs are updated.

Finally, Guardian Lens does not directly benchmark its classifier
against peer-model divergence methods such as RAVEN or VISTA. Such a
comparison would answer a different question and remains useful future
work.
