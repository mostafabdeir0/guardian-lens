\begin{abstract}

Can a black-box auditor recover which of several controlled decision
policies generated a vision-language model's behavior?

Guardian Lens studies this question using three researcher-induced
profiles---neutral, visual-cue-bound, and generalized---in a fixed-weight
vision-language model. The benchmark combines matched visual
counterfactuals, quantitative allocation decisions, an explicit efficiency
cost, and a transparent behavioral classifier calibrated before held-out
evaluation.

A nearest-centroid auditor was fit on six calibration scenes and frozen
before evaluation on 12 new held-out scenes comprising 432 blinded model
calls. The auditor recovered 33 of 36 scene-profile blocks, corresponding
to 91.7\% held-out accuracy and a macro-F1 score of 0.915. Cue-bound
target-emblem effects exceeded neutral behavior by 38.15 allocation points,
while generalized prioritization persisted despite a 20\% efficiency
penalty. All three classification errors occurred on distractor scenes in
which cue-bound behavior was designed to be observationally similar to
neutral behavior.

These results provide a controlled demonstration of behavioral system
identification from black-box multimodal observations while also exposing
an explicit identifiability boundary. Guardian Lens evaluates
researcher-induced inference-time behavior and does not establish the
presence of hidden learned objectives, genuine preferences, deception, or
trained sleeper-agent behavior.

\end{abstract}
